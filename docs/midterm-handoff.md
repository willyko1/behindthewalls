## Website coverage

The prototype demonstrates filtering, linked selections, measure changes, details on demand, accessible labels, and guided onboarding using real historical aggregates. The public methodology identifies data sources, normalization, deduplication, reporting bias, privacy, missing geography, and limitations.

The project connects to Undercurrents through essential housing conditions hidden behind apartment walls. It aims at the Gallery criteria of **Understanding** (reports versus distinct addresses), **Usefulness** (questions renters and organizers can explore), **Design** (coordinated readable views), and **Transparency** (shared source, frozen data, and method). These are intended criteria, not a claim of City acceptance.

## Demo route

1. Begin with the season: 341,824 requests at 41,102 distinct reported addresses.
2. Choose October, then January. All eight months remain visible.
3. In January, select the Bronx: 27,176 complaints at 5,232 reported addresses.
4. Switch to addresses. Brooklyn leads the five boroughs with 5,884 addresses despite fewer complaints (21,945) than the Bronx.
5. Select Brooklyn, then return to the season. Explain why monthly distinct-address counts cannot be added.
6. Copy the view link, refresh it, and use Back/Forward.
7. Open Sources & method. Explain why complaints are not confirmed violations or risk rates.
