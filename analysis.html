Reading the data

The count is part of the story.

01

Reports ≠ households.

A single address may generate many requests. Repeat reporting can reflect different residents, different problems, or an unresolved issue. This data cannot distinguish them.

02

Visibility is uneven.

Access to 311 and willingness to report influence what appears. Unreported experiences remain hidden. These are reported complaints, not confirmed violations.

03

Totals ≠ risk.

Borough counts have no housing-stock or population denominator. They are not comparable risk rates. No weather data was joined; a seasonal peak does not establish a temperature effect.

What is included?

341,824 NYC 311 service requests with complaint type HEAT/HOT WATER, created October 1, 2025 through May 31, 2026 inclusive. Source-local timestamps; the end boundary is June 1 exclusive. Snapshot retrieved October 7, 2026 and recovered unchanged from the public Observable prototype.

How are addresses counted?

An address key combines borough with the incident address, trimmed and converted to uppercase. Each key counts once in the selected month or once across the entire season. Full-season distinct addresses are precomputed from the snapshot, never added from monthly distinct counts. Formatting inconsistencies can affect the count; addresses are not a count of people, households, or verified buildings.

What is missing from the map?

Seventeen requests lack latitude/longitude; those with known boroughs stay in borough totals. One request has an unspecified borough and stays in citywide totals but is not mapped. The map uses simplified official borough boundaries. No individual addresses, coordinates of requests, or request identifiers are exposed.

What can this project tell us?

It shows patterns of reported experiences across time and boroughs. It cannot establish confirmed violations, affected households, causes, or whether repairs occurred. It was designed for NYC renters, with tenant organizers and community-board housing committees as a secondary audience.

“Behind the Walls” connects to Undercurrents by making often-hidden essential housing problems visible through public reports. It aims to support the Gallery criteria of Understanding, Usefulness, Design, and Transparency. It has not been submitted to the City.
